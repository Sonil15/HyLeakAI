How this ranking is calculated

Real output · site_suitability_ranking.csv

score = 0.50 · capacity − 0.30 · seal risk − 0.20 · heterogeneity, rescaled to 0–100

Each criterion is min–max normalised across the 1,000 realisations first, so the weights compare like with like. Capacity is a benefit; seal risk and heterogeneity are penalties, so they subtract. The weights are AHP-derived from stated pairwise judgments (consistency ratio 0.008). Use the presets above the chart to see how much the ranking depends on them.

Assumptions behind these numbers
  • Weights (0.54 / 0.30 / 0.16). Derived by the Analytic Hierarchy Process from pairwise judgments stated in src/ahp_weights.py, with a consistency ratio of 0.008 against the 0.10 acceptance threshold. The criteria follow the capacity / containment / reservoir-quality split used in storage screening. AHP formalises this team's judgments rather than an expert panel, which is why the stability sweep matters: across 5,000 nearby weightings, 26 sites stay in the top decile every time while individual ranks move about 40 places.
  • Seal risk is the peak caprock pressure margin, (Pmax − Pinit) / (Pfrac − Pinit). Pfrac uses a configured reservoir depth and fracture gradient, held fixed. Margin 1.0 would mean the configured fracture pressure was reached; across these 1,000 realisations the peak margin only ranges 0.43–0.79, so none approach it. 1.0 is not an approval threshold and does not replace a site-specific geomechanical study.
  • Mean log-permeability is excluded on purpose: its correlation with mean porosity here is 0.9995, so a fourth term would double-count one signal.

Across 5,000 weightings sampled around them, the top decile holds a 79% mean overlap and 26 sites appear in it every time, while individual ranks move about 40 places. The fault swarm replaces the histogram

Today the Monte-Carlo ensemble is a histogram of 25 probabilities, an abstraction of something inherently spatial. Draw the sampled faults where they are, coloured by risk, and the physics reads itself: the dangerous ones sit on the plume front, near the pressure ridge. Hover any segment on the slab above. The worst-case fault is extruded up through the caprock, because that vertical path is the leak.

The fault is not in the simulation. It is overlaid on the predicted fields, which is exactly what makes a live sweep of unknown fault properties affordable in the browser.

04 / BUILD

How the service is delivered

One container on Google Cloud Run: a FastAPI service runs the U-Net surrogate and the XGBoost screening model, and serves this interface from the same origin, so there is one URL and no cross-origin setup. It scales to zero when idle, so a cold request pays a short model-load before the first result. The full dataset stays out of the browser; the page carries the 1,000-realisation ranking inline and requests only held-out simulation IDs and compact assessment results.

01

Ship a field pack, not a dataset

Export ~24 test-set simulations from the Kaggle kernel, where the data already lives , as quantised uint8 sprite sheets of pressure and saturation. 24 sims × 60 steps × 128² × 2 fields is trivially small once PNG-compressed, and that is the entire field payload the frontend ever needs.

02

Run XGBoost in the browser

Dump the booster to JSON and walk the trees in ~150 lines of TypeScript. Then the fault Monte-Carlo is interactive: drag a fault, re-sample the ensemble, watch probabilities move at frame rate. That interaction is impossible against a Python round-trip and it is the single strongest moment available.

03

Port the Darcy flux to JS

fault_leakage_flux is closed-form. Reimplemented in JS it is a handful of lines, and it keeps the physics, not just the ML, live under the user's hands. Guard it with the same monotonicity self-checks src/leakage/labels.py already runs.

04

Keep the U-Net optional

The surrogate is the one piece that genuinely wants a GPU. Precompute its predictions into the same field pack and expose the simulator-vs-surrogate toggle the Streamlit app already has, the 99.0% PR-AUC retention is a result worth showing, not hiding.

05

Vite + TypeScript, canvas over chart libraries

Every visual here is a canvas and a few hundred lines of arithmetic. A charting library would fight the projection, the colormaps and the ribbon, and would still not draw the slab. Reach for one only if a plain line chart appears, and it probably will not.

Status of the data on this page

REAL, the 1,000-site atlas, its scores, ranks and criteria, read from outputs/site_suitability_ranking.csv.

MOCKUP, the reservoir fields, the fault ensemble and the attribution bars. The source dataset is not on this machine, so these are procedural stand-ins that demonstrate the visual language and the interaction, not model output. Nothing on this page should be quoted as a result.
