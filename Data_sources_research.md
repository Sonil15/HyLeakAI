# HyLeakAI — Data Sources & Build Plan

**Setup:** MacBook Air M1, 8 GB RAM, 8 cores, 4 GB free disk, no fan.
**Deadline:** basic prototype in 2 days, better prototype in 5 days.

---

## 1. The core problem

No public dataset contains hydrogen leakage measurements. Volve, F3, and every other open oil & gas dataset have zero hydrogen data and zero leakage labels. Underground hydrogen storage has only a handful of pilot projects worldwide (e.g. Underground Sun Storage, Austria) and none publish continuous leakage monitoring.

So you cannot train a supervised model directly on public data. You generate your own training labels with a physics simulation, using real geology from Volve to set the parameter ranges.

That is the technical contribution of the project.

## 3. Key physics numbers

### Hydrogen vs methane

| Conditions | μ(H₂) µPa·s | μ(CH₄) µPa·s | ratio | ρ(H₂) kg/m³ | ρ(CH₄) kg/m³ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 300 K, 1 bar | 8.94 | 11.25 | 1.26 | 0.1 | 0.6 |
| 340 K, 100 bar | 9.83 | 14.51 | 1.48 | 6.8 | 61.7 |
| 350 K, 200 bar | 10.20 | 17.98 | 1.76 | 12.5 | 119.4 |
| 370 K, 350 bar | 10.89 | 23.25 | 2.13 | 19.4 | 175.7 |

Hydrogen is only **1.3–2.1× less viscous** than methane. The big difference is **density — about 9–10×**.

This drives the model design: hydrogen escapes mainly by **floating upward (buoyancy / gravity override)**, not by flowing fast sideways. Your simulator needs gravity handled properly; viscous mobility contrast is secondary.

Reproduce:

```python
from CoolProp.CoolProp import PropsSI as P
for T, p in [(300,1e5), (340,1e7), (350,2e7), (370,3.5e7)]:
    h = P('V','T',T,'P',p,'Hydrogen')*1e6
    m = P('V','T',T,'P',p,'Methane')*1e6
    print(f"{T}K {p/1e5:.0f}bar  H2={h:.2f}  CH4={m:.2f}  ratio={m/h:.2f}")
```

### Where hydrogen actually goes

Ranked by what the literature reports:

1. **Leakage through old / badly sealed wells** — most likely engineered leak path
2. **Residual trapping** — hydrogen stuck in pores, up to ~41 % residual saturation in aquifers
3. **Dissolution + diffusion into brine** — H₂ diffusivity in water ~5×10⁻⁹ m²/s, roughly 3–4× methane's
4. **Microbial consumption** — methanogens and sulfate reducers eat hydrogen
5. **Caprock capillary breakthrough** — least likely; H₂ and CH₄ wettability are comparable and caprock stays water-wet

**Target old wells.** Best supported by research, cheapest to model (a leaky well is a 1D conduit, not a 3D fracture problem), and Volve has well trajectory and completion data.

## 4. Compute limits

| Model | One run | 1,000 runs |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3D + coupled geomechanics, 10⁵ cells | 1–4 h | 6–24 weeks ❌ |
| 3D flow only, 10⁵ cells | 15–45 min | 10–31 days ❌ |
| **2D slice, ~5×10³ cells** | **2–10 s** | **1–3 h** ✅ |

**2D only.** 500× faster and fits in 8 GB easily.

Two hard constraints:
- **Disk is 98% full (4 GB free).** Docker needs ~2 GB, DuMux needs 5–15 GB to build. Neither fits. Free up 20 GB before starting.
- **The M1 Air is fanless.** Sustained load throttles 30–40 % after a few minutes.

Handle geomechanics as **post-processing** — compute Mohr–Coulomb failure stress from the simulated pressure field on mapped fault planes. Roughly 3× cheaper than a coupled solve and fine for a risk score.

For later (not the hackathon): **OPM Flow** is free, open source, supports hydrogen storage natively, and reads ECLIPSE decks — the direct path from the real Volve model to a proper 3D H₂ simulation. [PFLOTRAN](https://docs.opengosim.com/tutorial/tut5/) has a worked hydrogen storage tutorial.

## 5. Two-day plan

### Day 0 — setup (30 min)

- [ ] Free up disk to at least 20 GB
- [ ] `pip install numpy scipy pandas scikit-learn xgboost matplotlib streamlit plotly CoolProp`
- [ ] Download SUHS-MRV + Volve ECLIPSE model

### Day 1 morning — simulator (4 h)

2D hydrogen/water flow simulator in Python, ~350 lines.

- 2D vertical slice, ~60 × 80 cells
- Reservoir rock below, seal above, one old well through it
- Two-phase Darcy, Brooks–Corey relative permeability, gravity on
- Fluid properties from CoolProp
- 3 cycles of 6 months injection / 6 months withdrawal

Outputs per run (these are your labels):
- % hydrogen lost
- days to breakthrough at top of seal
- lateral plume extent
- peak pressure at the well

Target **under 10 s per run**. If slower, shrink the grid.

> **Fallback if stuck by lunch:** single-phase pressure solver + a buoyancy rule for hydrogen movement. Less realistic, but you have working numbers by 2pm. A finished ugly pipeline beats a beautiful half-pipeline.

### Day 1 afternoon — generate dataset (2 h)

- [ ] Vary 6–8 inputs: permeability, porosity, seal thickness, seal permeability, injection rate, well seal quality, depth, temperature
- [ ] Set ranges from the Volve model — this is what makes it credible
- [ ] Latin hypercube, **300 runs**, ~1 h compute
- [ ] Save one CSV: 8 input columns + 4 output columns

### Day 2 morning — train (2 h)

- [ ] Gradient boosting (XGBoost or `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`) for each of the 4 outputs
- [ ] 300 rows × 8 features trains in under 5 seconds
- [ ] Feature importance chart — strong demo moment, tells a story
- [ ] R² on a held-out 20 %

Use gradient boosting, not deep learning. With 300 rows it beats a neural net and trains instantly. Deep learning goes on day 4.

### Day 2 afternoon — dashboard (4 h)

- [ ] Streamlit app, sliders for the 8 inputs
- [ ] Live risk prediction as sliders move
- [ ] 2D heatmap of hydrogen migration from a pre-computed run
- [ ] Reservoir Health Score — scale predicted loss % to 0–100
- [ ] SUHS-MRV time series for the operations view
- [ ] Practice the 3-minute pitch

**End of day 2:** real Volve data, physics simulation, 300-run dataset, trained model, interactive dashboard, health score. Complete prototype.

## 6. Days 3–5

Pick two or three. Not all.

**Day 3 — data**
- [ ] Microbial hydrogen loss as a decay term (one line, easy, sounds good)
- [ ] 1,000+ scenarios overnight instead of 300
- [ ] Add a fault, compute slip risk from the pressure field as post-processing

**Day 4 — models** (best value first)
- [ ] **Small U-Net CNN**: permeability map + injection rate → leakage heatmap. ~10 min training on CPU, most demo-friendly output you can build.
- [ ] **LSTM**: forecast pressure from the Volve history. Ties back to the real dataset.
- [ ] **PINN** (stretch): only after the CNN works. The PDE in your loss must be the same one your simulator solves, or it fails silently.
- [ ] **GNN**: skip. High effort, low payoff, your data isn't graph-shaped.

**Day 5 — polish**
- [ ] Economics: hydrogen loss cost, compressor energy, ROI vs traditional monitoring
- [ ] 3D view in Plotly (extrude the 2D slice — looks 3D, costs nothing)
- [ ] Early warning alerts on predicted risk
- [ ] Baseline comparison: "pressure-threshold monitoring catches it at day 40, we predict at day 5"
- [ ] Write down limitations honestly

## 7. Demo notes

- **Pre-compute everything.** Never run a simulation live during judging. Load results from CSV.
- **Say "physics-based synthetic training data."** Then explain that no public H₂ leakage data exists — that's *why* you generated it.
- **Cut features, not the pipeline.** Data → simulation → model → dashboard working end to end beats a great simulator with no UI.
- **Close everything else.** 8 GB fills fast and the fanless Air throttles when hot.
